# ChatGPT dictation

Select a live `openai-codex` model, then tap the microphone beside Send. Capture starts as soon as the microphone is available, without waiting for OpenAI's connection. A bounded audio buffer on the phone preserves the opening words while WebRTC connects, then streams them in order directly to OpenAI. Transcripts return directly to the phone's data channel. The Mac exchanges connection metadata using its Codex login; it does not upload audio or receive transcripts before Send.

```text
Phone ── connection offer ──→ Mac ── subscription authorization ──→ OpenAI
Phone ←──────────────────── connection answer ────────────────────────┘
Phone ═════════════════════ audio directly ═════════════════════→ OpenAI
Phone ←═══════════════════ transcript directly ═══════════════════════┘
Phone draft ── user taps Send ──→ Mac chat session
```

The text field stays visible and shows incoming words while recording, above the compact Cancel, waveform, Stop, and Send toolbar. Starting or stopping dictation does not change editor focus: an already-open keyboard stays open, and a closed keyboard is not opened automatically. Cancel restores the original draft; Stop restores the normal toolbar immediately, including model controls, the enabled Fast mode indicator, and slash commands. Send stops capture, moves the current text into a local chat bubble marked “Transcribing…”, and clears the composer. Trailing words update that bubble in place. Pi receives one prompt when the catch-up wait completes. Neither that update nor eventual delivery takes focus away from your next draft. The waveform grows from the right, scrolls continuously, and follows microphone volume, not transcription progress. At the left edge, bars taper into small round dots and fade out before reaching the boundary. The first use still requires browser microphone permission. The audio processor is prepared before recording when possible.

Stop ends microphone capture and keeps the live text as an editable draft, without changing the keyboard state. The recording status disappears; no finishing message or confirmation warning is shown. Before Send, typing or starting text composition freezes the current text. After Send, typing belongs to a separate draft: it neither cancels the queued message nor adds to it. Otherwise trailing words keep arriving. After Stop, Send reuses the ongoing catch-up window rather than starting a new one. Once recognition has finished or you have edited the text, Send submits the draft normally. There is no extra confirmation button. Only a tap on Send authorizes sending; recording alone never sends. Editing early can omit words that have not appeared yet. Automatic catch-up reduces that risk when sending, but is not proof that every word has arrived. Before you edit or send, Escape or changing sessions discards the unfinished preview and restores your original draft. Empty results preserve the draft silently. Assistant speech, assistant text, and tool requests from the voice service are ignored, never played or sent to the chat agent.

## Account and privacy

The Mac needs a current ChatGPT sign-in saved by Codex in `CODEX_HOME/auth.json`, or `~/.codex/auth.json` by default. Pi Remote uses that account, which may differ from Pi's selected account. Expired credentials require a fresh Codex login; keychain-only credentials are not supported. Durable credentials remain on the Mac.

This subscription route uses the separate `gpt-live-1-codex` speech/conversation model, not the selected reasoning model. OpenAI performs recognition in the cloud, not offline on the phone. Pi Remote does not persist recordings or send audio through its host or optional relay. There is no host transcript sideband or file-upload fallback. Claude and API-key-only OpenAI models do not show the microphone.

## Timing and cleanup

Captured audio stays buffered on the phone until the data channel is open and OpenAI sends `session.started`. A connected WebRTC transport alone does not establish recognition readiness: live probes sent entire sentences before that event without receiving text. The existing ten-second connection deadline also bounds this readiness wait; a timeout stops capture with an error and preserves the original draft rather than sending it. Playback remains at the original sample rate. Waiting protects opening words but can increase latency when OpenAI starts slowly. See the [startup latency measurements](research/dictation-startup-latency.md).

This route has no verified immediate transcription-flush command. Send waits for the phone's buffered audio to drain, then allows 2.5 seconds for recognition to catch up and at least one second without changed transcript text. Each change extends that quiet window. This fallback requires recognition to advance after capture stopped; stale text alone is not treated as completion. A user turn-final event received after the audio buffer drains can complete earlier, after a minimum 1.8-second drain period, provided no partial text remains. These signals are heuristics, not a guaranteed end-of-audio acknowledgment.

The wait shows “Transcribing…” without opening the keyboard. You can cancel queued delivery or tap Stop to keep a draft instead. Stop-then-Send reuses elapsed catch-up time. If the eight-second catch-up window expires after you tap Send, Pi Remote sends the words recognized so far, even if the ending is incomplete. No second confirmation is needed. Your next draft stays untouched. Stop alone remains quiet, with no timeout notice. Empty recordings never send an existing draft accidentally. Connection delays add to delivery time; they no longer block capture. The [direct-browser probes](research/phone-direct-dictation.md) measured complete speech about 2.1–2.3 seconds after Stop, not guaranteed phone performance.

Capture requires HTTPS or localhost, WebRTC, AudioWorklet, and microphone permission. The phone-only buffer holds at most 30 seconds of mono audio in memory, fails visibly instead of overwriting unsent samples, and is released on cancellation or completion. Audio is replayed at its original rate, not accelerated. The host advertises `voiceInput: webrtc-buffered`; older hosts leave the microphone unavailable until upgraded. The browser stops capture after two minutes. Cancel, Escape, session/model changes, leaving the page, and host disconnects stop the microphone and close the browser connection. Editing, including the start of text composition, freezes incoming updates before they can overwrite your changes. The pending bubble owns the dictated text after Send, so late results cannot refill the composer or change your next draft. Unsent bubbles are local to this tab and do not survive a reload. Connection/provider failures cancel queued sending and preserve any recognized words as a draft with a warning. Untouched previews are not saved as session drafts until finalized; editing saves them locally.

The browser sends `session.close` directly and waits up to one second for acknowledgement before closing its peer. The host cancels pending signaling and expires its local reservation after 150 seconds; it does not open an audio/transcript sideband to force remote closure. Remote retirement cannot be confirmed if the browser disappears or setup is cancelled before a usable data channel exists.

## Verification

Run `npm run test:voice`. It drives the real isolated host and browser UI with synthetic microphone audio and simulated external SDP/WebRTC. It checks direct-channel transcripts, outgoing host-message shapes, absence of transcript traffic before Send, waveform behavior, capture while signaling is held, no speech output before both data-channel and provider readiness, queued Send retaining buffered opening audio, readiness timeout preserving the draft, late readiness after cancellation, audible buffered output after readiness, the compact recording bar, restored model controls on Stop, no automatic text focus, live previews above the recording toolbar, retained editor focus, edits during recording and queued delivery, distinct Stop and Send actions, catch-up Send during recording and after Stop, delayed trailing words without a final event, and stalled or continuously changing transcripts sent once at the deadline after explicit Send, immediately editable drafts with no post-Stop status message, trailing words, freezing on edits and text composition, Send without a confirmation step, late-result isolation, silent empty results, cancellation restoring the original draft, session/model isolation, public errors, SDP bounds, disconnect cleanup, and compatibility during host upgrades. Preview screenshots cover 320px and 390px phones and a desktop viewport.

For real browser-to-OpenAI verification, explicitly run `npm run test:voice -- --live-file /path/to/synthetic-speech.wav`, using a short or long WAV ending with “The orange bicycle is parked beside the library.” This uses the host's existing Codex subscription login and opens three real WebRTC calls. It checks speech with signaling deliberately delayed while capture starts, a silent timeout, and Send during recording. It checks that the draft is editable immediately and the expected final words arrive within the measured startup backlog plus six seconds, then edits the draft to freeze incoming updates. It also checks that the silence case reached provider readiness rather than merely disappearing after a startup failure. It reports authorization, connection, provider readiness, first-text, preview-update, buffer-drain, editable-draft, and complete-text timings separately, records whether editing happened before confirmation, confirms close acknowledgements, and verifies that no transcript crosses the host command channel before Send. The final call explicitly sends the recognized sentence to the isolated simulated agent, not a real Pi session, and reports the catch-up delivery time. Include a long recording: short samples alone did not expose the missing-final-event problem described in [turn-completion verification](research/dictation-turn-completion.md). Per-call JSON evidence includes transport counters and audio energy, transcript events, numeric startup metadata, the final draft, and Send state. The test records no credentials or SDP in these JSON files and uses synthetic speech only. This verifies editing without waiting for confirmation, not a fix for OpenAI's missing final-turn event.

Physical iPhone/Safari capture and performance still need device verification. OAuth success does not establish unlimited access, zero extra cost, or a stable public API contract.
